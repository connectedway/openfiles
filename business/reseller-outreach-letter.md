**Subject:** OpenFiles SMB stack — exploring reseller / partnership opportunities for embedded OEMs

Dear [name],

I'm Richard Schmitt, founder of Connected Way. We develop and license **OpenFiles**, a fully independent, production-grade SMB client and server stack for embedded systems.

OpenFiles powers SMB connectivity across **HP's multi-function printer product line** and has been shipping in volume for years. The stack is independently developed — not derived from Likewise, Samba, or any open-source SMB project — and supports modern SMB2/3 with encryption, multi-channel, DFS, and full Active Directory integration. It's portable across RTOS, Linux, Android, and desktop OSes.

In parallel, we use OpenFiles as the engine behind **GridLock** (gridlockvpn.com), our own Android product — currently the only native (JNI) Android SMB implementation that integrates DFS, Active Directory, and the Storage Access Framework, and the only Android-native SMB *server*. GridLock is how we keep OpenFiles sharp: every new feature in modern Android storage, every SMB protocol revision, gets exercised against real-world consumer workloads before it lands in our OEM releases.

The combination matters for partners: OpenFiles isn't maintenance-mode legacy code. It's an actively-developed embedded SMB stack with both a tier-1 OEM shipping it in volume and a consumer product driving its evolution.

We're now looking to expand OpenFiles into additional OEM accounts through reseller and sales-representative partnerships with firms who already have established procurement relationships in the embedded, imaging, and printer OEM space.

The arrangement we'd propose is straightforward:

- **Non-exclusive reseller agreement** for OpenFiles licensing into your existing OEM accounts
- **Commission on license revenue** (terms negotiable, market-standard)
- **Support, maintenance, and custom engineering** handled directly by Connected Way, with revenue flowing direct to us at standard rates

If OpenFiles looks like a fit for your portfolio, I'd welcome a 30-minute discovery call.

## Editorial notes

- **HP is named once, factually**, in an architectural-claim context — not name-dropping flex. Informal approval exists; explicit confirmation pending.
- **GridLock is the second credibility pillar.** Active product development on a consumer surface answers the "where have you been all my life?" question and shows OpenFiles isn't maintenance-mode.
- **"Combination matters for partners" line** ties OEM proof + consumer-product proof of life together. Without this line, the two credibility statements feel disconnected.
- **Call to action is small** — 30-minute discovery call. Standard low-commitment ask.
- **Length is intentionally under 400 words.** Resellers read the first paragraph; everything after is to close the sale on the discovery call.

## Variants to consider

- **Boutique sales reps / manufacturer's reps** — likely best fit; this draft works as-is.
- **Large electronics distributors (Avnet, Arrow, Future)** — may need a longer technical summary plus reference to OEM volume figures; consider attaching the data sheet earlier.
- **Industry-adjacent consultancies** — possibly more relationship-led; the cold outreach version may be less effective than a warm intro.
